What it does

Unlike the Dragon, which attacks whatever is closest, the Dragon Rider prefers defences and will fly past collectors, huts and storages to reach them. Combined with air movement, that produces a unit which flies directly toward the things that matter, ignoring both walls and distractions.

The usual corollary applies: once every defence is destroyed, it reverts to nearest-target behaviour like anything else.

How to use it

Because they self-direct toward defences, Dragon Riders need much less steering than most armies.

  • Deploy as a group toward the defensive core rather than trying to funnel them.
  • Support with spells for the anti-air. Their weakness is the same as every air unit's, and rage or freeze on the key Air Defence is usually what decides the attack.
  • Bring clean-up. Since they ignore non-defensive buildings until the end, an army of only Dragon Riders can leave a scatter of huts standing.

How to beat it

Strong, well-spread anti-air, and defensive Clan Castle troops that can engage air units. Because Dragon Riders go straight for defences, a base that keeps its anti-air deep and mutually covering forces them to fly through sustained fire to reach anything.

Frequently asked

How is the Dragon Rider different from the Dragon?

The Dragon Rider targets defences specifically, while the Dragon attacks whatever building is nearest. That makes Dragon Riders far more self-directing, since they fly straight to the defences instead of eating the perimeter first.

Do Dragon Riders need a funnel?

Much less than a ground army, because they fly and they seek defences directly. What they do need is a plan for the anti-air, which is what actually stops them.
