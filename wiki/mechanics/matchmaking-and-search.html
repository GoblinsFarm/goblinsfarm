Matchmaking and the Search Screen

Mechanics

Farming

Updated: 16 September 2026 · By the Goblins Farm team

Multiplayer matchmaking pairs you with an opponent based principally on trophies, not on Town Hall level, base strength or how much loot they are holding. That single fact explains most of what players find frustrating about searching, and points directly at how to search better.

Trophies decide who you meet

When you tap Attack, the game looks for a village near your trophy count that is currently attackable, meaning it is not shielded, not under Guard and not currently being attacked by someone else. It does not filter by Town Hall level, and it does not care whether the base is a maxed fortress or an abandoned wreck.

Everything downstream follows from this. If you have pushed trophies well beyond your base's actual strength, matchmaking will keep handing you bases that outclass your army, because trophies are the only thing it is matching on. Conversely, if you deliberately drop trophies, you will meet weaker, poorer opponents, more of whom have quit. This is the trophy-versus-loot trade-off in a single sentence, and it is explored properly in trophies and leagues.

What nexting costs you

Skipping a base charges a small gold fee, and that fee escalates the longer you keep skipping within a single search session. In pure resource terms it is trivial next to the loot you are hunting.

The real cost of nexting is time, and, if you are honest about it, attention. Twenty minutes of tapping next to find one good base is twenty minutes you did not spend doing anything else, and the tenth base looks a lot like the second when you have stopped paying attention. Search discipline matters more than search cost:

  • Set a number before you start. Decide your minimum acceptable loot before the first base appears, and hold to it. Deciding case by case is how you end up attacking a mediocre base out of impatience.
  • Train while you search. Never sit on the search screen with an empty queue. The army should always be cooking.
  • Judge in three seconds. Loot preview, collector state, Town Hall position. If it fails, next. Do not study bases you are not going to attack.

Why the same bases keep appearing

Two reasons, usually. First, at unusual trophy ranges, or at odd hours in your region, the pool of attackable villages near your trophy count is genuinely small, so you cycle through it. Second, the search widens its range the longer it looks, which is why the twentieth base often looks nothing like the first.

If you are seeing the same handful of bases, you have two levers: move your trophy count to enter a different pool, or search at a different time of day when a different part of the world is online and unshielded.

What you cannot control

It is worth being clear about the limits, because a lot of folk wisdom claims otherwise:

  • You cannot search for a specific resource. Dark elixir hunting is not a search filter; it is skipping until you find drills worth hitting.
  • You cannot search for a specific player or revenge someone who has not attacked you.
  • You cannot make inactive bases appear more often. They are simply villages in your trophy range whose owners stopped playing, and pushing into ranges those players occupied is the only lever you have.
  • Base layout quality is not a matchmaking input. A perfectly designed base and a random pile of buildings at the same trophy count are equally likely to be offered to you.

What you can control is your trophy range, the time you search, your loot minimum, and how quickly you make the skip-or-attack call. That is the whole game on the search screen.

Frequently asked

Does Town Hall level affect who I am matched against?

Not for multiplayer matchmaking, which pairs you primarily on trophies. Town Hall level does affect how much storage loot is available to you when there is a mismatch, and it matters a great deal in war matchmaking, which uses an entirely different system.

Does skipping bases cost anything?

Yes, a small gold fee per skip that escalates during a long search session. It is negligible compared to the loot you are looking for. The genuine cost of nexting is your time.

How do I find inactive bases?

There is no filter for them. Inactive bases are simply villages in your trophy range whose owners quit, so the practical approach is to sit in a trophy range where more players have stopped playing and to skip quickly with a firm loot minimum.
