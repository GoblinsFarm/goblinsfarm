Spell Mechanics: Radius, Timing and Stacking

Mechanics

CombatCore mechanic

Updated: 18 September 2026 · By the Goblins Farm team

Spells are the smallest part of an army by housing space and the largest part by outcome. A correctly timed Rage turns a stalled push into a three-star; the same spell dropped four seconds early does nothing at all. The mechanics that govern them are simple, and almost nobody reads them.

The two spell families

Regular spells come from the Spell Factory and are paid for in elixir. Dark spells come from the Dark Spell Factory and are paid for in dark elixir. They share the same spell housing space, but dark spells occupy less of it, which is why a mixed set is usually better value than a pure one.

Broadly, regular spells do the big, expensive things: mass healing, mass damage boosts, opening walls, freezing defences. Dark spells do the small, cheap, surgical things: poisoning defending troops, disabling a single defence, speeding up a push, spawning a distraction. A well-built army usually carries a couple of large regular spells and one or two cheap dark ones for a specific job.

Radius and placement

Every spell affects a circle centred exactly where you tap, with a fixed radius. Three consequences that decide most attacks:

  • Drop ahead of the army, not on it. Troops move. A Rage centred on your troops right now is centred behind them two seconds from now. Aim where the front of the push will be.
  • Cover the group, not the star player. A spell that covers eight troops is worth four times one that covers two. If the army has spread out, the spell is already partly wasted, which is another reason to deploy in a controlled line.
  • Overlapping does not multiply. Two Rage spells on the same troops do not double the effect. Overlapping is for extending the covered area or the duration, when a push must cross a long stretch of base.

Timing

The duration clock starts on impact, not on first effect, so a spell dropped before the troops arrive is a spell partly spent on empty ground. The general timing rules:

  • Rage goes down when the troops are already engaged with something worth killing quickly, not while they are walking.
  • Heal goes down when the damage is already happening, ideally where the army will remain for the full duration. Healing a group that is about to walk out of the circle wastes most of it.
  • Freeze is reactive, not planned. You hold it for the moment a specific defence starts shredding your army, most often an Inferno Tower or a Scattershot, and you drop it then.
  • Jump goes down before the troops reach the wall, because it takes a moment to open and the army does not wait.
  • Poison goes on defending Clan Castle troops as soon as they are clumped, during the lure, not during the main attack.

The habit that improves attacks fastest: hold your spells. New players dump every spell in the first fifteen seconds because it feels productive. Attacks are lost at the sixty-second mark, and that is when the spells should still be in your hand.

Building the spell loadout

Work backwards from the failure mode of your army, not forwards from a list.

  • If the army dies to splash damage, bring healing.
  • If the army survives but runs out of time, bring haste or rage.
  • If the army stalls at a specific defence every time, bring the counter to that defence, usually freeze.
  • If the army cannot get into the core, bring jump or more wall breakers.
  • If the army dies to the defending Castle, bring poison and lure properly.

Then, and this is the part people skip, watch the replay of the attack that failed and check which of those five it actually was. The right spell is a diagnostic result, not a preference.

Frequently asked

Do spells stack in Clash of Clans?

Not usefully in terms of power. Two of the same spell on the same troops do not double the effect. What overlapping achieves is a larger covered area or a longer covered window, which is what you want when a push has to travel across a wide base.

When should I drop a Rage spell?

When the troops are already engaged and the extra damage will land on something that matters, and slightly ahead of them so they move into the circle rather than out of it. Rage dropped while an army is walking across open ground is mostly wasted.

Are dark spells better than regular spells?

They are different tools. Dark spells cost dark elixir and take less spell housing, which suits cheap, surgical jobs such as poisoning a defending Castle. Regular spells do the heavy lifting. Most strong armies carry a mix rather than committing to one factory.
