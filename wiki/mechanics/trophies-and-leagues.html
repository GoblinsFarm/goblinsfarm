Trophies and Leagues: The Farming Trade-off

Mechanics

ProgressionFarming

Updated: 16 September 2026 · By the Goblins Farm team

Trophies measure competitive standing and decide who you are matched against. Leagues, which are bands of trophy count, decide how large a bonus you collect for winning an attack. The two pull in opposite directions for anyone who cares about resources, and choosing where to sit is one of the few genuinely strategic decisions in the game.

What trophies actually do

Trophies do two jobs. They are the game's ladder, so they determine your league and your standing. And they are the input to multiplayer matchmaking, so they determine who you meet, as covered in matchmaking and the search screen.

The second job is the one that matters for resources. Your trophy count is a dial that sets the difficulty and the wealth of the opponents the game offers you. Turn it up and you meet stronger, richer, more active players. Turn it down and you meet weaker, poorer, more frequently abandoned ones.

The league bonus, and why it complicates everything

If leagues only gated matchmaking, the answer would be simple: drop as low as tolerable and farm inactive bases forever. But winning an attack also pays a league bonus, a flat award of gold, elixir and dark elixir that scales steeply with league. In the highest leagues, the bonus alone can be worth more than the loot you steal.

So the decision becomes an actual calculation:

  • Low leagues. Cheap opposition, many inactive bases with full collectors, minimal bonus. Income comes almost entirely from stolen collector loot. Works brilliantly with the cheapest possible armies, and tolerates a low win rate because you were not counting on the bonus anyway.
  • High leagues. Strong opposition, few inactive bases, large bonus per win. Income depends on winning, and since the army itself is free, what a failed attack costs you is the search fee and several minutes. Profitable only if your win rate is genuinely high.

The break-even sits wherever your win rate and your patience say it does, and it moves as your base grows. A player who can three-star reliably belongs higher than one who cannot, regardless of Town Hall level.

Deliberate trophy dropping

Dropping trophies on purpose, sometimes by dropping a single troop and quitting, is a long-established farming tactic. It works because it moves you into a matchmaking pool with more abandoned bases and cheaper opposition, and because trophies you are not using are not doing anything for you.

Some caveats worth knowing before you do it:

  • It is slow. Getting down takes many deliberate losses, and the game will keep handing you trophies for successful defences on the way.
  • It is reversible but tedious. Climbing back up costs real attacks.
  • It affects your clan. Some clans have trophy requirements, and low trophy counts can affect where you sit in clan listings.
  • It does not help war. War matchmaking does not use trophies, so dropping does nothing for your war matchups.

Season resets and where to settle

At the end of each competitive season, trophy counts above a threshold are pulled back down, and rewards are handed out based on the league you finished in. This mostly concerns players near the top of the ladder; if you are farming in the middle, resets barely register.

For everyone else, the practical advice is boring and correct: settle at the highest league where you still win comfortably with a cheap army. If you are skipping endlessly because nothing has loot, move up. If your cheap army keeps failing against real defences, move down. Test it deliberately for a week rather than guessing, and let the actual loot per hour decide.

Why there is no league table on this page

Every other stat table on this wiki is read from Clash of Clans' own game files. League trophy thresholds and bonus values are not in the files we read, so publishing a table of them would mean transcribing numbers from somewhere else and presenting them with the same confidence as the verified ones.

We would rather say that plainly. The trade-off described above does not depend on the exact figures, and the current bonus for your league is shown in game on the league badge, which is both authoritative and up to date.

Frequently asked

Should I drop trophies to farm?

Only if you are struggling to find profitable bases where you are. Dropping puts you among weaker, poorer, more frequently abandoned opponents, which suits very cheap armies. It also costs you the league bonus, which in higher leagues can exceed the loot you steal, so it is a trade rather than a free win.

What is the best league for farming?

The highest one where a cheap army still wins comfortably. That is a different league for different players and it changes as your base grows, so test it: farm a week in one league, a week in the next, and compare actual resources gained per hour rather than trusting a rule of thumb.

Do trophies affect clan war matchmaking?

No. War matchmaking uses an entirely separate system based on the composition and strength of the participating rosters, so trophy dropping has no effect on the wars your clan is given.
