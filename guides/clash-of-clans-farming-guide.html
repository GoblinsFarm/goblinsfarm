Clash of Clans Farming Guide, Armies, Leagues, and Loot

Updated: 24 August 2026

Farming in Clash of Clans means attacking for resources instead of trophies. Done right, it is the difference between waiting days for an upgrade and starting it tonight. This guide covers the fundamentals that have held up for years: what to train, where to farm, what to attack, and how shields work, so you can build an efficient loop regardless of your Town Hall level.

Farming armies: cheap and fast wins

The core principle of a farming army is simple: profit per hour, not stars per attack. A powerful army that takes long to train and costs a fortune has to steal a fortune just to break even. A cheap army that trains in minutes can attack far more often and keeps almost everything it grabs.

  • Barch (barbarians plus archers). The all-time classic. Barbarians soak damage, archers shoot over walls at exposed collectors and storages. Trains fast, costs little, works at nearly every Town Hall.
  • Goblin-heavy armies. Goblins move fast, deal double damage to resource buildings, and strip exposed loot before defenses react. Ideal for dead bases and sloppy layouts, fragile against anything that fights back.
  • Mass cheap troops with a few wall breakers. When the loot sits one wall layer deep, a handful of wall breakers turns a surface attack into a storage attack for minimal extra cost.

Whatever you run, the test is the same: if the army costs more to train than an average attack returns, it is not a farming army.

League choice: the core trade-off

Where you farm matters as much as what you train, and the league you sit in decides both the opposition and the bonus you collect. The trade-off in broad strokes:

Lower leaguesHigher leagues
Dead basesCommon, easy pickingsRarer
Loot bonus per winSmallMuch bigger
OppositionWeak, cheap armies sufficeStrong, attacks cost more
Search qualityHit or miss, lots of nextingFuller storages on active bases

Neither end is simply better. Lower leagues suit attacking collectors with throwaway armies. Higher leagues suit players who can reliably win and want the league bonus stacked on top of the stolen loot. Many experienced farmers deliberately drop trophies to sit below their natural ceiling, because relaxed farming at volume beats stressful farming at the limit. Experiment: if you are skipping forever without finding loot, move; if every base crushes your cheap army, drop.

Dead base hunting

Dead bases are the farmer's best friend. When a player quits, their collectors and drills keep producing until they cap, and all of it sits in the collectors rather than the better-defended storages. You can spot one at a glance:

  • Collectors and mines visibly full or overflowing
  • An inactive or default clan castle and no league badge activity
  • Collectors placed outside or at the edge of the walls, unprotected

Against a dead base, a wave of goblins or barch takes the whole haul in under a minute with barely any losses. The catch is finding them: dead bases are handed out by the matchmaker at random, so hunting them means pressing next, a lot. That skipping costs search gold and, more importantly, your time and patience.

Collectors vs storages

Every base offers two kinds of loot. Collector loot sits in mines, pumps, and drills, usually near the outside, lightly defended, and a large share of it is stealable. Storage loot sits in the big storages, normally centered behind walls and defenses, and only a fraction of what the owner holds is available to you. The practical rule: attack collectors on dead and lazy bases with cheap surface armies, and only commit to a storage push when the loot preview shows numbers that justify the army cost. Learn to read the loot preview before you deploy a single troop, it is the most important skill in farming. Shields and guard basics

You cannot be attacked while shielded, and you get a shield when an attacker does sufficient damage to your base. Farmers exploit this rhythm rather than fight it:

  • Attacking does not break your shield immediately. You can attack a limited number of times under a shield before it ends early, so use those attacks deliberately.
  • Village Guard. After a shield ends you get a short guard period where you still cannot be attacked. Chain attacks inside it.
  • Spend before you log off. The best defense is having nothing worth stealing. Queue upgrades and dump loot into walls so defenders find empty storages.
  • Do not overpay for shields. Losing a defense while your storages are empty costs you almost nothing and hands you free protected farming time.

Putting the loop together

An efficient manual session looks like this: train a cheap army, search while it cooks, skip until the loot preview clears your personal minimum, attack collectors or commit to storages as the numbers dictate, collect, requeue the army, repeat. Between sessions, keep builders busy so loot never idles in storage. Run that loop consistently and upgrades that felt weeks away arrive in days.
