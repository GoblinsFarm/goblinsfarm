Clash of Clans Farming Bot, How Loot Farming Actually Works

Updated: 24 August 2026

Farming in Clash of Clans is the least interesting part of the game: press "find a match", eyeball the loot, hit next, next, next, finally attack, collect, retrain, repeat, for hours, because the next upgrade costs millions. A farming bot exists to do exactly that loop for you. This guide walks through what an automated farming session actually looks like, how targets and stop conditions work, and what you can realistically expect.

Anatomy of a farming session

Here's the loop Goblins Farm runs, step by step. It's the same loop you'd run by hand, just tireless.

  1. Search. The bot opens the attack menu and starts searching for opponents, reading the screen with computer vision (template matching plus OCR) to know exactly which state the game is in.
  2. Read the loot. On each candidate base, it OCRs the available gold, elixir, and dark elixir shown on the scout screen.
  3. Skip or attack. If the loot is below your configured thresholds, it hits "next" and pays the small search cost. If the base clears the bar, it attacks. This is the single most important behavior in loot farming, more on it below.
  4. Deploy. Troops go down and the battle plays out. Farming attacks aren't about three-starring; they're about extracting loot efficiently with a cheap army.
  5. Collect and repeat. Back in the village, the bot collects, waits for the army to retrain, and searches again.

The whole thing runs inside the free BlueStacks emulator on your PC or Mac. Nothing touches the game client and nothing talks to Supercell's servers, the bot only looks at pixels and simulates taps, like a very patient human. (Setup takes a few minutes; see the BlueStacks setup guide.)

Loot thresholds: skipping dead bases is the whole game

Anyone who has farmed manually knows the temptation to attack a mediocre base just because you're tired of pressing next. Bots don't get tired, which is precisely their edge. Set your thresholds, say, a minimum combined loot that makes an attack worth your army cost and training time, and the bot will skip a hundred dead bases in a row without flinching until one clears the bar.

Where you set the bar is a real decision:

  • Thresholds too low: you attack constantly but each attack barely pays for itself. Lots of activity, little net loot.
  • Thresholds too high: every attack is juicy, but you burn time and search costs skipping. In leagues where fat bases are rare, you can spend more time nexting than attacking.
  • The sweet spot depends on your league and army cost. Start moderate, watch a session, and adjust the sliders. Because the bot applies the rule perfectly every time, even small tuning changes show up clearly in results.

Farm to target, then stop

Most bots historically ran until you turned them off. Goblins Farm is built around the opposite idea: you tell it what you're farming for.

Need 8 million gold for the next Town Hall upgrade and 2,000 dark elixir for a hero level? Set those numbers with the target sliders and start the session. The bot farms until your storages hold the targets, then stops on its own, "farm to target, then stop" is a first-class mode, not an afterthought.

This matters for two reasons. Practically, farming past your target is wasted effort, storages cap out and overflow loot evaporates. And from a risk standpoint, a bot that runs in purposeful sessions and then goes quiet looks a lot more like a human player than one grinding 24 hours a day, every day. It's one of several habits that reduce your footprint, though, to be plain about it, no habit makes botting safe. Realistic expectations: loot per hour

Here is where most bot marketing lies to you, so let's not. We won't quote you a loot-per-hour number, because any fixed number would be made up. Your actual rate depends on your league (which controls how rich the bases you're offered are), your army composition and training time, your thresholds, and plain luck with the matchmaker. The same bot, same settings, can have a fat hour and a lean hour back to back.

What a bot genuinely changes is not some magic multiplier, it's consistency. It executes your strategy every single attack, never gets bored, never "just takes" a bad base, and keeps going long after you'd have put the phone down. Loot accumulates because the loop never stops running, not because each attack is superhuman.
