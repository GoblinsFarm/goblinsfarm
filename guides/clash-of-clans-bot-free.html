Free Clash of Clans Bot, What You Actually Get

Updated: 24 August 2026

You searched for a free Clash of Clans bot, and we sell a paid one, so let's get the bias on the table right away. This page is still going to give you the straight version: free options do exist, one of them was genuinely great for years, and for a certain kind of person free is the right call. But free bots come with costs that are not on the price tag, and you should know what they are before you spend a weekend on setup.

First, the disclaimer that applies to every bot at every price: automation violates Supercell's Terms of Service, free or paid, and no tool can honestly promise you will not be banned. Our ban risk page covers that in detail.

The free options in 2026

MyBot.run

MyBot.run is the free option worth talking about. It is open source, Windows-only, and was for years the most capable CoC bot anywhere at any price: attack strategies, loot filters, donations, the lot. If someone tells you they farmed to max Town Hall with a free bot, this is almost certainly the bot they mean.

The catch is the present tense. MyBot is maintained by volunteers, and that volunteer activity has stalled. The code still exists, the forum still exists, but the update cadence no longer keeps up with the game's update cadence, and that gap is where free bots die. More on this in our MyBot alternative guide.

Auto clickers

The other free route is a generic auto clicker or macro recorder: record some taps, replay them on a loop. These are free and they technically run, but they cannot see the screen. They will happily tap the attack button while a maintenance popup is open, deploy troops on a base with no loot, and get stuck the first time anything unexpected appears. Making one do useful farming in Clash of Clans is a project in itself, and the result is fragile. As a category, they are macros, not bots.

Why free bots break

This is the core of it, and it is not a knock on the developers. Every serious CoC bot works by computer vision: it recognizes buttons, loot numbers, and screens by their appearance. When Supercell ships an update that moves a button, restyles a menu, or adds a new popup, the bot's picture of the game no longer matches reality, and it stops working. This happens many times a year, forever.

So a bot is not a product you download once, it is a subscription to maintenance, whether or not money changes hands. With a paid tool, you paid someone to be on the hook for that maintenance. With a free tool, you are hoping a volunteer feels like doing it this month. When they do not, the bot stays broken, and "free bot" quietly becomes "no bot."

The hidden costs of free

  • Setup hours. Free tools assume you will read forum threads, edit config files, and troubleshoot emulator quirks yourself. Budget an evening if things go well, a weekend if they do not.
  • Broken weeks. After each game update, you wait. Maybe days, maybe weeks, maybe forever. Farming that stops every time the game changes is farming that mostly does not happen.
  • Sketchy downloads. The official open-source repo is one thing. But the ecosystem around free bots, repacked builds, forum mirrors, and especially "cracked" versions of paid bots, is a classic malware delivery channel. A free bot that comes with a credential stealer is very expensive.
  • No support. When it misbehaves, the fix is you, a search box, and an old forum thread.

When free is the right choice

Honestly? If you are a tinkerer, take free. If you enjoy the puzzle, are comfortable in config files, run Windows, and treat the bot itself as the hobby, MyBot when it is working costs nothing and teaches you a lot about how these tools work. Same if you just want to experiment with the idea of botting before committing a cent. Nothing on this page argues against that.

When paid wins

Paid wins when what you actually want is the loot, not a hobby project. The whole value of a farming bot is that it runs reliably while you are asleep or at work, and reliability is precisely the thing free options no longer deliver. A maintained tool means someone ships a fix when the game updates, an auto-updater installs it, and your farming resumes without you reading a forum.
