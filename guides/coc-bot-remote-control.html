Control Your CoC Bot From Your Phone

Updated: 24 August 2026

A farming bot runs on a computer at home. You are, most of the day, not at that computer. That gap is where most botting anxiety lives: is it still running? Did it hit the target? Did an update break something at 2 a.m.? Goblins Farm closes the gap by turning your phone into the bot's remote control, push notifications for everything that matters, with Stop, Start, and Status buttons right inside the notification. Here's how to control a CoC bot from your phone, what you'll actually get pinged about, and how the whole thing works without an account or a server.

What lands on your phone

Once linked, your phone receives Clash of Clans bot notifications for the events worth knowing about:

  • "Farm started", a session kicked off, either from the desktop or from your phone.
  • Loot summaries, what the session collected, so you can watch the gold, elixir, and dark elixir totals climb toward your targets without opening the game.
  • Target reached / session stopped, the bot hit the loot targets you set and stopped itself.
  • Errors, if the emulator disconnects or something else goes wrong, you find out immediately instead of discovering a stalled bot hours later.

Each notification carries action buttons: Stop halts the bot, Start launches a session, Status asks the bot to report what it's doing right now. Tapping a button on your phone controls the app on your computer, from anywhere with an internet connection.

How it works: ntfy.sh, no account, no server

The plumbing is ntfy.sh, a free and open publish-subscribe notification service. The model is simple: messages are published to a named topic, and anything subscribed to that topic receives them as push notifications. No account needed on either end, and nothing for you to host.

Goblins Farm generates a private, random topic name for your installation. The topic name is effectively a shared secret, it isn't listed anywhere, and only devices that know it can read your notifications or send commands. The bot publishes status messages to the topic and listens on it for the Stop/Start/Status commands your phone's buttons send back. That's the whole architecture: no port forwarding on your router, no VPN back into your home network, no third-party dashboard with a login.

Setup: scan one QR code

  1. Install the free ntfy app on your phone (Android or iOS).
  2. Open Goblins Farm's Settings on your computer and find the notifications section, the app shows a QR code encoding your private topic.
  3. Scan the QR code with the ntfy app. Your phone is now subscribed.

That's it. The next session start will produce your first push. If you ever want a fresh topic, say a device that knew the old one is gone, generate a new one in Settings and re-scan.

What this looks like day to day

Start a session from work. BlueStacks and Goblins Farm are running at home; you tap Start in a notification on the train, and by the time you're home the loot targets are met and the bot has stopped itself.

Reclaim your village when you want to play. Feel like an evening of real attacks? Tap Stop from the couch. No walking to the computer, no yanking the emulator out from under a mid-attack bot.

Get pinged when storages hit target. Set your gold and elixir targets with the sliders, start the bot, and forget about it, the "target reached" push is your cue to log in and spend the loot on upgrades. The same applies to Builder Base sessions, which stop on their own targets or when the night village storages are full.

Sleep through the errors, but see them at breakfast. If something breaks overnight, the error push timestamps exactly when, and a Status tap tells you where things stand before you decide whether it needs you at all.

Why remote control matters more for bots than you'd think

There's also a practical botting-hygiene angle. A bot that runs unattended forever because stopping it is inconvenient is a bot that farms long past its targets. Cheap, instant remote stopping means you actually run the bot the way you intended: to a target, then done. It pairs with Goblins Farm's automatic stop conditions to keep sessions bounded. That's no guarantee of anything, all automation violates Supercell's ToS and the risk is never zero, but a bot you can supervise from your pocket is a bot you stay in control of.
