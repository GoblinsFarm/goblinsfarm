Clash of Clans Bot for Mac, Yes, It Finally Exists

Updated: 24 August 2026

Search for a Clash of Clans bot as a Mac user and you'll find a decade of forum threads that all end the same way: "sorry, Windows only." MyBot? Windows. Practically every legacy bot, script, and tool the community ever built? Windows. Mac players have been told to dual-boot, rent a Windows VPS, or just farm by hand. This page is about the exception: Goblins Farm is a native macOS app, signed, notarized, Apple Silicon ready, that farms Clash of Clans on your Mac.

Why Mac users never had a bot

It's not malice, just history. The classic bots were built on Windows-only automation frameworks (MyBot, for example, is written in AutoIt, which simply doesn't exist on macOS) and drove Windows-only emulators. Porting any of them would have meant a rewrite, and volunteer projects don't do rewrites for a minority platform.

Two things changed. First, BlueStacks shipped BlueStacks Air, a proper macOS build of the world's most popular Android emulator, running well on Apple Silicon. Second, Goblins Farm was built cross-platform from day one: the same computer-vision engine, template matching and OCR reading the emulator screen, simulated taps going back in, runs on both Windows and macOS. Nothing about the approach is Windows-specific, so the Mac isn't a port; it's a first-class platform.

Signed and notarized, why that matters

Here's the part that's genuinely rare in this niche. Most bot tooling, when it exists for Mac at all, arrives as an unsigned binary that modern macOS actively fights: Gatekeeper throws the "cannot be opened because the developer cannot be verified" screen, and you're left right-clicking, digging through System Settings, and running xattr commands in Terminal just to launch the thing, while wondering what else an unsigned mystery binary might be doing.

Goblins Farm's macOS app is code-signed and notarized: it goes through Apple's developer signing and notarization pipeline, which scans the app before it's cleared to run. In practice that means:

  • You open the .dmg and drag the app to Applications like any normal Mac software.
  • No "unidentified developer" warnings, no Terminal workarounds.
  • The app you run is byte-for-byte the app that was signed, tampering breaks the signature.

To be clear about what notarization is not: it's Apple's malware check, not an endorsement of botting, and it says nothing about ban risk (more on that below). But among CoC bots, a space historically full of sketchy unsigned executables, a notarized app is very rare, and it's the difference between software that respects your machine and software that asks you to disable its protections.

Setting it up on your Mac

The whole stack is: BlueStacks runs Clash of Clans, and Goblins Farm watches and controls BlueStacks from outside. Outline:

  1. Install BlueStacks Air (free) from bluestacks.com and let it finish its first-run setup.
  2. Install Clash of Clans inside BlueStacks from the Play Store and sign in to your village.
  3. Download the Goblins Farm .dmg from goblinsfarm.com/#download, open it, and drag the app to Applications. It opens cleanly, see above.
  4. Run the setup wizard. On first launch, Goblins Farm walks you through connecting to the emulator and auto-detects ADB (the Android Debug Bridge it uses to send taps), no manual paths or terminal configuration.

From there it's the same app as on Windows: set loot thresholds and farm-to-target sliders for the main village, enable Builder Base mode if you have that license, and optionally hook up phone notifications and remote start/stop via ntfy.sh. The app auto-updates: it checks for new releases on launch and prompts you when a fix ships.

Apple Silicon

M-series Macs work. BlueStacks Air runs on Apple Silicon, and Goblins Farm drives it there just as it does everywhere else. If your Mac is from the last several years, you're covered.

The same honest caveat, Mac edition

A nicer install experience doesn't change the rules of the game. Automation violates Supercell's Terms of Service on macOS exactly as it does on Windows, and the risk of account action is never zero. Goblins Farm only reads the screen and simulates taps, no modified client, no memory editing, no talking to Supercell's servers, which puts it in the lowest-risk category of bot design, but lower risk is not no risk.
